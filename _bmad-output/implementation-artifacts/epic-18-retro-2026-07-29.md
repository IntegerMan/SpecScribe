## What Went Well

### Both spikes overturned their own premises

The only thing a spike is for.

- **18.1** set out to write an artifact-coverage map and instead found a **live identity defect**: a CIS/TEA/BMB-only
  repo reported itself as BMad Method and was served BMM's entire glossary. Every first-party module except GDS
  prefixes its skills `bmad-`; **GDS was correct by coincidence** — the one module whose skill prefix happens to
  equal its module code. It also found a second, install-order-dependent regression: with `cis` or `tea` ahead of
  `bmm` in manifest order, BMM was demoted and every BMM command suggestion vanished portal-wide while
  `IsMethodPresent` still reported `True`.
- **18.3** was asked whether to parse `bmad-index-docs`' `index.md` as a blurb source. Answer: **don't** (D0). The
  field SpecScribe wanted is the least stable part of the file, and `index.md` turned out to be a **contested
  filename across at least three BMad skills** — the filename does not identify its producer.

A spike that says "no" is worth as much as one that says "yes." This epic produced one of each.

### 18.1's review failure was fixed *inside the same epic*

18.1's empirical basis was a scratchpad probe that was deleted after the run, leaving none of its external claims
falsifiable from the repo — the review called that fatal. **18.3 answered with a 14-row evidence ledger**: URL,
retrieval date, commit SHA, byte counts, sha256, and the verbatim sample bytes reproduced inline so the finding
survives the scratchpad's deletion. 18.5 then re-pinned TEA's contract the same way, per-file SHAs travelling with
the fixtures rather than in prose.

That is intra-epic learning, not a retro action item — the strongest kind.

### Nobody regenerated the golden fingerprint blindly

Epic 5's retro asked for this. Three separate stories established causality instead:

- **18.2** ran a worktree causality experiment — copied its seven files onto the baseline, the constant did not
  move, proving the story was not the cause.
- **18.4** bisected with `git archive` and proved the failure existed **without any of its code**, then let the
  concurrent Story 20.8 own the regeneration and recorded **reciprocal attribution in both directions**.
- **18.6** built an isolated tree, overwrote only its own eight files, and got 2625/0/3 twice while the shared tree
  showed 23 failures — all confined to the five files a concurrent session was mid-write in.

18.6's is the sharpest: the shared tree was **not compiling** at one point, `Charts.cs` referencing a symbol a grep
found defined moments later. The file changed between two reads. Documented as the transient mid-write hazard
rather than panicked over, and nothing of the other session's was reset.

### Live-browser verification caught what the suite structurally could not

- **18.4** found **two defects no assertion then written would have caught**: a carried-report link that climbed out
  of `ideas/` and back into a nonexistent path, and double-prefixed breadcrumbs. It also caught a regression it had
  itself caused — suppressing orphan `forged-idea.md` pages left `BuildReferenceMap` handing out 18 dead links on
  `code-map.html`. Whole-site audit: **778 hrefs, 0 dead.**
- **18.6** measured the CIS dashboard at **104px, exactly the sum of its visible children, 0px trailing gap** —
  proving the panel omission left no collapsed residue, which no unit test can see.

### Decisions went into ADRs, unprompted

Three ADRs, two of them explicitly citing the CLAUDE.md trigger. ADR 0020 (a module may declare non-markdown
sources) and ADR 0021 (a foreign artifact may be carried verbatim as a gated dead-end leaf) are both genuine
cross-cutting contract changes that a story note would have buried.

**18.2 followed a ratified ADR over its own task list.** ADR 0015 was ratified by a *concurrent session while 18.2
was mid-implementation*, gaining Decisions 1a–1d, 2a–2d and 4a–4e. Per CLAUDE.md ("a ratified ADR is the
authority"), the implementation followed the ratified text and the story file was updated in flight to match.

### Honesty about interpretation boundaries became a real type

`CoverageTier` (`Rendered` / `Summarized` / `Unsupported`) closes an open question that had sat unanswered in
**both** the PRD and `SPEC.md` — *"How should coverage tiers be communicated so users understand interpretation
boundaries?"* — and had zero implementation anywhere in `src/`. One classifier, no surface spells a tier itself,
the word always carried alongside the colour (UX-DR17). Both open-question lines were struck through with the
answer recorded inline.

## Challenges

### 1. Story 18.6's code review ran one of three adversarial layers

Blind Hunter and Edge Case Hunter both failed on the session's API usage limit; the owner chose to proceed rather
than wait. Acceptance Auditor found no AC violations and two patch findings were applied. But this is the only
story in the epic whose review is **structurally thinner than its siblings'**, and it is the story that changed a
dashboard omission rule. **Owner decision at this retro: schedule the two missing layers.**

### 2. Scope-by-File-List failed exactly where Epic 5 predicted — and we have the receipt

Story 18.2's review found `IsModulePresent` made public and `ForCode` added **inside 18.2's primary file**, both
self-attributing to **Story 18.5** in their own doc comments, and neither in 18.2's File List. A File-List-scoped
review sees neither. The deferred-work entry states the carry-forward plainly:

> *"when a story's review is scoped by File List, symbols a sibling added to the same file are invisible to
> **both** reviews unless one of them records the handoff."*

Epic 5 action item #4 asked for content-attribution-by-hunk as a second tier. It was still `open`, and it bit
again. **Written into CLAUDE.md at this retrospective.**

A sub-item rides with it: `ForCode` calls `BuildContext(csv)` with the `diagnostics` sink **omitted**, so ADR 0015
Decision 1c's label cross-check demotes silently on the Test Artifacts path.

### 3. Two ADRs sat Proposed, and one deferred to an ADR that does not exist

ADR 0020 §5 defers its enrichment-only constraint to **ADR 0019** — which is claimed-but-unwritten by *two*
stories at once (18.3's "LLM-generated artifacts are enrichment-only inputs" and 22.3's IR-projection
reconciliation). Epic 26 (Sonar findings) and Epic 27 (coverage reports) both ingest non-markdown external data and
will cite 0020; had it still been Proposed at their start, they would either build on an unratified decision or
re-litigate it. **Both ratified at this retrospective; ADR 0019's contention is seated as an action item.**

### 4. Documentation lagged the epic by the whole epic

Six stories shipped module coverage. Neither the README's framework table nor the in-portal About-SDD page
mentioned modules at all until a separate pass **after** the epic closed. That pass surfaced a real accuracy trap:
the intuitive way to write it up — *Modeled / Interpreted / Recognized* — is **wrong**, because
`ModuleContext.ModuleForCode` maps only `bmm` and `gds` to modeled values. **Test Architect is `Unmodeled`
exactly like `cis` and `bmb`**: it gets interpreted test artifacts *and* no glossary, no planning-doc set, and an
omitted artifact-coverage panel. Only reading the mapping caught it; the shipped behaviour alone suggested a
middle tier that the identity layer does not have.

The same pass found a second defect: `CoverageTiers.Description(Summarized)` read *"…and shows that **here**"* —
"here" meant the Test Artifacts page, and went silently wrong the moment About-SDD began rendering the same
strings as its tier legend. A shared vocabulary string had named its own location.

### 5. A cross-surface honesty defect shipped and is still open

`AdapterDiagnosticCategory.Informational` collapses to `"warning"` on the webview Problems wire —
`Commands.SerializeDiagnostics` has no `Info` arm. 18.2's "FYI, nothing to do" unmodeled-module notice therefore
arrives in VS Code as a **Warning** on every run, contradicting the owner constraint that the unmodeled state must
not read as an error.

Correctly deferred as pre-existing (Story 4.2's notice was the first emitter). But note **why it was invisible**:
the HTML surface maps `Informational` correctly to `diag-info`, and the HTML surface is the one 18.2 verified live.
*Live verification on the surface you built does not protect the surface you didn't.*

### 6. `.claude/launch.json` drift, third epic running

18.4 added `ideas-18-4` / `ideas-18-4-jsoff` pointing at a per-session `AppData/Local/Temp` scratch path that will
not exist for anyone else who checks out the repo. 18.6's review separately caught a **port collision** — its own
entry and a concurrent sibling's both claiming 8114.

## Significant Discoveries

**None that invalidate a downstream epic's plan** — but three that downstream epics must inherit:

1. **ADR 0020 is now the governing contract for non-markdown ingestion**, and Epics 26 (Sonar findings) and 27
   (coverage reports) are its next two consumers. Both must honour the four conditions — module-declared, exact
   filename, directory-scoped, presence-gated — and the schema-version gate as **ratified** (accept-higher-and-warn),
   not as currently coded. Epic 27's coverage report is the harder case: it is not module-declared in a
   `workflow.yaml` at all, so it will need an explicit extension of 0020 rather than a straight application.
2. **ADR 0021 establishes that the portal may contain a page it did not compose.** Any later story surfacing a
   foreign HTML artifact — a coverage report, a vendor export — inherits the script-free / external-origin-free /
   size-capped gate, the dead-end-leaf framing, and the exclusion from SPA/webview capture.
3. **Test Architect is `Unmodeled`.** Any surface, document, or story that presents TEA as a tier between "modeled"
   and "recognized" is misleading about the identity layer. This already caused one documentation defect.

### The new golden rule was exercised during this retrospective

Worth recording, because it happened by accident and validated the convention within minutes of writing it.

While closing out, `GoldenContentFingerprint` failed in the shared tree, asking for `b41c3315…` against a
committed constant of `a649bd17…`. **No rendering code had been touched since the last verified-green run.**
Under the old habit the move would have been regenerated on the spot.

Applying the rule instead: `git archive HEAD` into the scratchpad, non-incremental build, run the gate.
**Pristine `HEAD` passed both golden tests.** The drift comes entirely from a concurrent session's *fifteen*
uncommitted rendering files (`Charts.cs`, `CodeMap*.cs`, `HierarchyExplorer*.cs`, `Requirements*.cs`,
`StatusStyles.cs`, `TraceabilityTemplater.cs`, `specscribe.css`, …). Correct action: **leave the constant
alone**; it is that session's to regenerate when their work lands.

Two incidental confirmations of things already in `CLAUDE.md`:

- The documentation pass's own constant (`9544578b…`) was **superseded** — commit `9421a8c` regenerated to
  `a649bd17…`, absorbing the doc pass's rendering deltas. Exactly the Story 18.4 ↔ 20.8 pattern: the later
  regeneration subsumes the earlier one, and reciprocal attribution is what keeps it honest.
- `SiteGeneratorAdapterTests.cs` was observed carrying `9544578b…` and, moments later, `a649bd17…` — the file
  changed between two reads. The same transient mid-write hazard Story 18.6 recorded. *Verify after every edit*
  earns its place in the conventions again.

## Key Takeaways

1. **A spike's job is to be wrong in public.** Both Epic 18 spikes overturned their own premises, and 18.1's
   overturning — a live identity defect nobody was looking for — reordered the entire epic.
2. **A review failure fixed inside the same epic is worth more than an action item.** 18.1's unreproducible
   evidence became 18.3's evidence ledger without waiting for a retrospective.
3. **File-List review scoping breaks precisely when the project's own working model succeeds.** Concurrent work on
   shared `main` is an accepted condition; the review convention had not caught up to it. Now it has.
4. **Verifying the surface you built does not protect the surface you didn't.** The webview `Informational` defect
   was invisible to 18.2's live verification because the HTML surface renders it correctly.
5. **Check the convention file before writing a convention.** Three of four "unwritten" rules were already written.
6. **Ratify decision records at the epic that authors them.** Two ADRs governing what the product may read sat
   Proposed while two downstream epics prepared to build on them.
